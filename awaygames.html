Why away days are scored differently

Every course has its own rating. A handicap is built from score differentials, and a differential is your gross total minus that course's rating — not minus par, and not minus Leeds's rating. Leeds Wike Ridge plays off 69, Forest Pines off 70. Get the rating wrong and every handicap built on the round is wrong with it, which is why each away day carries its own figure in the tracker rather than inheriting the home one.

Par and stroke index change too. Hollins Hall is a par 71, 36 out and 35 back, and its stroke index runs in a completely different order from Leeds. Both were read off the cards themselves, and the Stableford points on every card were recomputed hole by hole from that index to confirm the transcription before anything was loaded.

Source. Hole-by-hole scorecards from Golf GameBook, transcribed into the society tracker and checked card by card — every printed Out, In, gross total and Stableford points figure reproduced from the raw hole scores before loading.
Net. Gross total minus the handicap played off that day, as recorded on the card. It is not adjusted between courses, so net scores compare within an away day rather than across them.
Handicaps. The figure in the NHS column is the society's own internal handicap — the one the player actually played off that day, and the one every net score here is worked out from. It is not their WHS index, which is a separate national figure held on the register for comparison.
