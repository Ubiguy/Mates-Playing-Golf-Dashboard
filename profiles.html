Reading these numbers

NHS is still the handicap. The big number on the left of every card is the player's NHS handicap — the society's own internal figure, sized so members compete fairly against each other, and the one they actually play off. Nothing else on this page changes that, whichever section a player appears in.

Two records, on one card, deliberately kept apart. A player can have two histories here and they answer different questions. The society record is worked out from the hole-by-hole scorecards in our own tracker. The England Golf record comes from that player’s own My England Golf export — their WHS index, the same thing as an England Golf index and referred to by both names here and on the handicap register, calculated by England Golf from cards submitted at their clubs. They are built the same way but from different rounds, so a number from one is not comparable with a number from the other, and they are never merged. A card shows whichever it has: both, one, or neither. Where a WHS figure is an older estimate rather than a loaded export it is marked est, exactly as on the handicap register, because both pages read the same figures. Score differential is a round's gross total minus the course rating — 70 at Forest Pines, 69 at Leeds — so it measures a round against the course rather than against par. Lower is better, and the averages here run over a player's whole record, not just this season.

Peak and low. Each card shows either the highest index a player has carried or the lowest, whichever is the more telling for their history, with the date it was reached. Net change runs from the start of their record to today: a minus sign is an improvement.

Sample sizes vary enormously. Tab Rafique's index rests on six rounds, Waseem's on forty, and several society records stand on a single card. Anything under four rounds is marked Thin record and greyed out, the same threshold the handicap register uses — those figures move by whole shots on one bad hole, so read them as placeholders rather than handicaps.
