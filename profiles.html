Reading these numbers

NHS is still the handicap. The big number on the left of every card is the player's NHS handicap — the society's own internal figure, sized so members compete fairly against each other, and the one they actually play off. Nothing else on this page changes that, whichever section a player appears in.

Two different sources, deliberately kept apart. The first group's cards are built from each player's own My England Golf export — their WHS index, the same thing as an England Golf index and referred to by both names here and on the handicap register, calculated by England Golf to the WHS (World Handicap System) method from cards submitted at their clubs. Each card is rebuilt from that player's own round history, so the figures move as new exports arrive; where no export has been supplied the card shows an earlier WHS record and is marked est. The other twenty-three have a society index, worked out here from the hole-by-hole scorecards in our own tracker. They are built the same way but from different rounds, so a number from one section is not comparable with a number from the other. Where a player in the second group has supplied their My England Golf export, their WHS index is shown on their card too; where only an earlier or estimated figure exists it is marked est, and where there is nothing the tile stays blank. Those three states match the handicap register exactly, because both pages read the same figures.

Score differential is a round's gross total minus the course rating — 70 at Forest Pines, 69 at Leeds — so it measures a round against the course rather than against par. Lower is better, and the averages here run over a player's whole record, not just this season.

Peak and low. Each card shows either the highest index a player has carried or the lowest, whichever is the more telling for their history, with the date it was reached. Net change runs from the start of their record to today: a minus sign is an improvement.

Sample sizes vary enormously. Tab Rafique's index rests on six rounds, Waseem's on forty, and several society records stand on a single card. Anything under four rounds is marked Thin record and greyed out, the same threshold the handicap register uses — those figures move by whole shots on one bad hole, so read them as placeholders rather than handicaps.
